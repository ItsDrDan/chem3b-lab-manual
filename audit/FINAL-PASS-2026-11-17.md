# Final pass (2026-11-17): accessibility, pedagogy, directions, accuracy, grammar, page layout

Uses the **current numbering** (Exps 1–15, two workshops). Per-file change lists (before → after) are in `audit/working-notes/final-pass/group1.md` … `group5.md`.

## What changed

- **About 190 text edits across all 20 source files.** Five reviewers read every line, and each change was checked against the diff. The scan of the diff found no change to any amount, concentration, PPE item, heating step or waste route. The only safety-related wording added was "In the fume hood:" on three Exp 3 steps, which matches that lab's existing hazard rule (decision 1 below).
- **Report Sheets now match the procedures.** Steps now tell students to record everything the Report Sheet asks for, in the same order and with the same labels. Materials lists name the items the steps use.
- **Accuracy fixes.** Highlights:
  - Exp 1 A.2: methane has no C–C bond.
  - Exp 2: the naming-rule example no longer gives away the answer to Q.1(d).
  - Exp 4: six compounds are tested, not five.
  - Exp 7: ammonium salt wording.
  - Exp 12: whey is never clear; the heavy-metal mechanism is corrected.
  - Exp 14: the solubility rule no longer contradicts itself.
  - Exp 15 A.2: students now change ribose back to deoxyribose before building the nucleotide.
  - Appendix E: "every chiral L-amino acid except cysteine is S".
  - The cellulose and amylase wording.
- **Questions no longer answered by a nearby figure or sentence.** Exp 9 Q.4c, Exp 10 Q.4 (samples 2–5), Exp 11 Q.1, Q.2 and Q.6, and Exp 13 (pepsin's optimum pH taken out of the intro).
- **Accessibility.**
  - Figure captions no longer depend on color alone or on "left/right".
  - "See below" is replaced with section or step names.
  - Minus signs and en dashes are consistent.
- **DNA lab (Exp 15).** Fewer instructor checks: A.1 (sugar models) and A.3 (stockroom base models) no longer have checks. A.2 (nucleotide) and A.4 (antiparallel strands) keep theirs.
- **Exp 4 A.3.** Students build the four C₄H₁₀O alcohols one at a time and draw each one, so a kit with 4 carbons is enough.
- **Front matter.** The waste table no longer mentions chromate tubes; the chromate test was removed earlier.
- **Tables stay on one page.**
  - The shared table code keeps every table that fits on a page in one piece, using keep-with-next on every row but the last.
  - Short text-only appendix tables are kept together the same way.
  - Report Sheet parts no longer each force a new page. They flow, and tables still don't split.
  - Before, 12 lab tables split across pages. Now only one does: Exp 2 D.4, four isomer boxes taller than a page. It breaks between isomers and repeats its header.
  - In the appendices, only the long tables and the tables with structure images still split.
- **Page layout.** Every page was checked on contact sheets. Each lab, the workshops and the appendices still start on a right-hand page. The manual is 289 pages.

## Needs a co-author or EHS decision (not changed)

**Waste routes and the four-container rule**
1. Exp 3 E.2 puts the bromine tubes in "direct sunlight", but Hazards says to keep the Part E tubes in the fume hood. "In the fume hood:" was added to C.2, C.3 and F.1 to match Hazards; revert this if those steps aren't meant to be in the hood.
2. Some routes fall outside the four containers:
   - "Trash": Exps 3, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10.
   - Drain: Exp 7's later rinses, Exp 8's fermentation tubes and Exp 12's whey.
   - "Broken glass box": Exps 10, 11 and 15.
3. Exp 5 Tollens': the tubes go to non-halogenated organic waste, but leftover reagent goes to aqueous waste. EHS should pick one route for ammoniacal silver.
4. Exp 7 amide-hydrolysis tubes, Exp 8 Seliwanoff (resorcinol in HCl), Exp 10 FeCl₃ tubes and filtrate, and Exp 15 base solutions in dilute HCl all go to non-halogenated organic waste. They are mostly aqueous, and the lab waste rule would send them to aqueous waste.
5. Front matter, Table 1: Tollens' tubes are listed under non-halogenated organic waste. Confirm this with EHS.

**Heat and flammables**
6. Exp 10: the hazard says to keep acetic anhydride away from hot plates, but A.3 heats it in a boiling water bath on a hot plate.
7. Exp 11 B.6 offers a hot plate for the ninhydrin-sprayed plate, but Hazards says to keep the spray away from hot plates.
8. Exp 12 D.2: the ninhydrin solvent isn't stated. If it is alcohol-based, it is heated in a boiling bath with no flammability warning.
9. Exp 12: 20% acetic acid is missing from Hazards.
10. Exp 14: nitrile gloves give poor protection against methylene chloride. The iodine solution's concentration is never stated.

**Amounts and bench checks**
11. Exp 9: A.2 uses 5 drops of each liquid lipid, but A.5 uses 10–15 drops in only 1 mL of CH₂Cl₂.
12. Exp 12:
    - B.3: NaOH may show little visible change.
    - B.5: chloride in egg white or tap water forms AgCl, which looks like denatured protein. Distilled water may be needed.
    - C.3: "2–3 mL" of acid is about 10 times what's needed, and the re-warming step has no temperature limit.
13. Exp 13:
    - The 50 mL of starch and 40 mL of amylase set aside are less than the 56 mL and 44 mL the procedure uses. A note says to refill from the stock bottle; about 60 mL and 50 mL are suggested.
    - Commercial α-amylase and the ethanol inhibitor need a bench test.
14. Exp 15:
    - Chamber: the lab lists a 600-mL beaker, but Exp 10 uses a 400-mL beaker.
    - B.7 variations 1 and 2 need a second strawberry and a buffer without detergent or without salt. Neither is in the materials.
