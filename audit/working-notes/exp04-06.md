# Experiment 4: Alcohols and Phenols

## 1. Inventory
- **Parts.**
  - A: model kit; skeletal formulas of ethanol, 2-propanol, t-butyl alcohol and salicylic acid; 1°/2°/3° classification.
  - B: odor, water solubility and pH of 4 alcohols + salicylic acid (solid) + unknown.
  - C: chromic acid oxidation test (6 tubes).
  - D: FeCl₃ phenol test (6 tubes).
  - E: identify the unknown.
- **Central objective:** classify alcohols and use the chromic acid and FeCl₃ tests to tell 1°/2° from 3° alcohols and alcohols from phenols.
- **Reagents as stated:**
  - Ethanol, 2-propanol, t-butyl alcohol (2-methyl-2-propanol) and cyclohexanol: 5 drops each (B, D) and 8 drops (C).
  - Salicylic acid: solid "about the size of a grain of rice" (B); "0.15% salicylic acid solution" (C, D; 8 and 5 drops).
  - "2% chromate reagent (2% K₂Cr₂O₇ in dilute H₂SO₄)": 2 drops per tube.
  - "1% FeCl₃ solution": 5 drops per tube.
  - Distilled water: 2 mL per tube in B.
  - pH paper.
- **Equipment:** test tubes (size never stated), rack, stirring rod, plastic pipet, beaker of ice water, spatula, model kit.
- **Heat/hood/UV/vacuum:** none. No hood is required. Ice water is used only if a tube gets hot.
- **Time (estimate):** A 15 min; B 25 min; C 20 min; D 10 min; E 10 min; total about 80–90 min. Bottleneck: shared chromate dropper bottle (6 tubes per student).
- **Report outputs:**
  - A.1/A.2 drawings + classification.
  - B table (odor, soluble, pH).
  - C table (color, formula, class, product).
  - D table (color, ±).
  - E.1 evidence table; E.2 name + structure.
- **Post-lab:**
  - Q.1 draw/classify 4 alcohols.
  - Q.2 thymol/menthol/resorcinol.
  - Q.3 [O] products (1-propanol, 2-butanol, cyclohexanol).
  - Q.4 how C + D separate 2°, 3° and phenol.
- **Textbook topic:** alcohols, phenols, alcohol classification, oxidation of alcohols, phenol acidity.

## 4. Science and calculations
- **Chromate chemistry (line 29).** The intro says yellow chromate is acidified to orange dichromate. The reagent, however, is stated to be K₂Cr₂O₇, which is already orange dichromate. Wording should match the actual reagent.
  - The equation 2 CrO₄²⁻ + 2 H⁺ → Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O is balanced: Cr 2/2, O 8/8, H 2/2, charge −2/−2.
- **Fig 5.** Products are correct: ethanol → acetaldehyde → acetic acid; 2-propanol → acetone; 2-methyl-2-propanol → NR.
- **Fig 1.** Thymol: OH at C1, isopropyl ortho, CH₃ at C5 relative to OH. This matches 2-isopropyl-5-methylphenol. Correct.
- **Fig 4.** The phenoxide "O⁻" charge is a very small superscript and is barely visible at print size (P3).
- **pH of 0.15% salicylic acid** (pKa 2.97, Ka 1.07×10⁻³):
  - 1.5 g/L ÷ 138.12 g/mol = 0.01086 M.
  - x² + 1.07×10⁻³x − 1.162×10⁻⁵ = 0 → x = 2.92×10⁻³ M → pH 2.54.
  - The intro's "pH about 2–3" is consistent.
  - Solubility check: 0.15% (1.5 g/L) is below about 2.2 g/L at 25 °C, so the solution is feasible.
- **Salicylic acid solid in B.2.** A rice-grain spatula tip is roughly 10–20 mg (estimate). 2 mL of water dissolves about 4 mg (0.2 g/100 mL), so the expected result is "insoluble" (solid remains). Saturated solution pH ≈ 2.4 (0.0159 M): x = 3.62×10⁻³.
- **Phenol pH "about 5–6".** For 0.1 M, pH = ½(10.0 + 1) = 5.5. OK.
- **Cyclohexanol solubility.** 5 drops ≈ 0.25 mL ≈ 0.24 g in 2 mL is about 12 g/100 mL, against a solubility of about 3.6 g/100 mL. Expected: two layers/cloudy, matching the intro.
- **Expected observations:**
  - C: ethanol, 2-propanol and cyclohexanol go blue-green; t-butyl alcohol stays orange (it may slowly change over long times); salicylic acid stays orange/brown (needs bench test).
  - D: only salicylic acid gives violet.
- **Risk of copying expected results.** The intro and Fig 6 give every expected color, and the report tables invite copying. Mitigated by the unknown.
- Pre-lab 4 answers: (a) blue-green, carboxylic acid; (b) blue-green, ketone; (c) orange, NR, because the 3° carbon has no H. All supported.
- Q.3 products: propanal → propanoic acid; 2-butanone; cyclohexanone. All supported.

## 3. Bench executability
- **B.3 / Instructor Notes (lines 58, 73): unsafe and unspecified heating.**
  - Mixtures containing methanol (bp 65 °C) and 1-propanol (bp 97 °C) go into a *boiling* (100 °C) water bath on a hot plate at the bench.
  - The tubes hold about 4.75 mL (3 + 1 + 0.75), with no tube size stated.
  - Expect methanol to boil vigorously and possibly bump, losing much of the 3 mL over 15 min (needs bench test). Mixture 1 may then become a hot H₃PO₄/salicylic-acid residue.
  - The hood is required only for dispensing, not for heating or wafting.
- **B.2 (line 70): one graduated cylinder for everything.** One 10-mL cylinder is listed for 5 alcohols + acetic acid, with no rinse instruction. Carry-over will mix odors (e.g., pentyl acetate's banana odor into later mixtures).
- **B.3: one small beaker for five mixtures.** Only "small (50- or 100-mL) beaker" (singular) is listed for the optional hot-water pour. Reusing it contaminates later odors.
- **C.3 (line 78): too little NaOH.** "1 mL … 10% NaOH": 2.5 mmol OH⁻ vs 1.5–1.9 mmol methyl salicylate. The phenol uses a second equivalent, so saponification may be incomplete and "until the oily ester layer disappears" may never happen in 30 min (STILL OPEN; needs bench test).
- **A.6 and C.5 (lines 66, 80): litmus endpoint is too high for precipitation.**
  - Blue litmus turns red at about pH 4.5–5. At pH 4.5:
    - benzoate (pKa 4.2) is only about ⅓ protonated;
    - salicylate (pKa 3.0) is only about 3% protonated.
  - Stopping at the first red litmus may give little or no white solid, contradicting the intro and Figure 3.
  - C.5's "about 20 drops in all" (≈2.7 mmol HCl vs 2.5 mmol NaOH) only barely overshoots (needs bench test).
  - No failed-result guidance (e.g., "if no solid forms, add 5 more drops and cool").
- **Tube volume in A.** 2 mL + 0.25 mL acid + about 1.75 mL NaOH (35 drops needed for acetic acid; see §4) + ≥1.6 mL HCl ≈ 5.6 mL. Tube size is not stated; a 13×100 mm tube would be about 70% full. P2.
- **C.3:** "You should still see two layers" tells students what to observe before they look (observe-before-react cue). P3.
- **Sequencing.** C.3 needs 30 min in the bath, but nothing tells students to start C while B heats. Total time may exceed a 3-h period (estimate).

## 4. Science and calculations
- **A.2 acetic acid:**
  - 5 drops ≈ 0.25 mL × 1.049 g/mL = 0.262 g ÷ 60.05 = 4.37 mmol in about 2.25 mL → 1.94 M.
  - pH = ½(4.76 − log 1.94) = ½(4.76 − 0.29) = 2.24. "Usually about 2–4" is OK.
  - NaOH needed: 4.37 mmol ÷ 2.5 M (10% w/v = 10/40.00 g/mol per 100 mL) = 1.75 mL ≈ 35 drops, i.e., about 4 additions of 10 drops. Feasible.
- **A.2 benzoic acid:**
  - Solubility 0.34 g/100 mL at 25 °C → saturated 0.0279 M.
  - [H⁺] = √(6.3×10⁻⁵ × 0.0279) = 1.33×10⁻³ → pH 2.9.
  - A 30 mg spatula tip (estimate) in 2 mL is 1.5 g/100 mL, so solid remains (correct).
  - At 100 °C (about 5.6 g/100 mL), 2 mL dissolves about 110 mg, so it dissolves fully, then crystallizes on cooling (correct).
- **B mole ratios** (acetic acid 1 mL = 1.049 g ÷ 60.05 = 17.5 mmol):

- **H₃PO₄:** 15 drops ≈ 0.75 mL × 1.685 g/mL = 1.26 g; × 0.85 = 1.07 g = 10.9 mmol H₃PO₄, plus about 0.19 g (10.5 mmol) water. That water slightly opposes esterification; minor.
- **C.3:**
  - 5 drops methyl salicylate ≈ 0.20–0.25 mL × 1.174 g/mL = 0.235–0.293 g ÷ 152.15 = 1.54–1.93 mmol.
  - NaOH = 1.00 mL × 2.5 M = 2.5 mmol.

  - Ratio NaOH : ester = 1.3–1.6, matching the earlier "1.3×".
- **C.5:**
  - Salicylic acid formed (if complete) = 1.54–1.93 mmol × 138.12 = 213–267 mg.
  - Solubility in about 5 mL ≈ 11 mg, so an obvious white solid if the pH is low enough.

  - Fig 6 (esterification): balanced.
  - Fig 7 (saponification of ethyl acetate): balanced.

  - Fig 2: correct.
