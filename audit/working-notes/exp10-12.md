## 4. Science and calculations
- **Table 2 formulas:** carbon counts recomputed — palmitoleic 1+5+2+7+1 = 16 ✓; oleic 1+7+2+7+1 = 18 ✓; linoleic 1+4+2+1+2+7+1 = 18 ✓; linolenic 2+2+1+2+1+2+7+1 = 18 ✓; saturated rows ✓. Melting points within normal literature ranges ✓.

- **Figure 2:** tripalmitin from glycerol + 3 palmitic acid, 3 H₂O ✓.
- **Figure 3:** oleic acid + Br₂ → 9,10-dibromo product ✓.
- **Part B drop-count arithmetic (L10-01):**
  - 1% (w/v) Br₂ = 10 mg/mL; one drop ≈ 0.05 mL → 0.50 mg = 3.1 µmol Br₂ per drop; 50 drops ≈ 156 µmol. (If "1%" is w/w in CH₂Cl₂, ρ 1.33: 13.3 mg/mL → 4.2 µmol/drop.)
  - One drop of a viscous oil ≈ 0.03–0.05 mL (needs bench test) × ~0.91 g/mL ≈ 27–46 mg.
  - Olive oil, iodine value ≈ 80 → 0.80 g I₂/g ÷ 253.8 = 3.15 mmol C=C/g → 85–145 µmol → **≈27–46 drops**.
  - Oleic acid, 1 C=C per 282.5 g/mol = 3.54 mmol/g (ρ 0.895) → 85–158 µmol → **≈27–51 drops**.
  - High-linoleic safflower oil, IV ≈ 140 → 5.5 mmol/g → 149–254 µmol → **≈48–81 drops (often "more than 50")**. High-oleic safflower (the common retail type) IV ≈ 90 → similar to olive oil.
  - Conclusion: oleic acid and olive oil are expected to be within ~10% of each other (indistinguishable by drop counting), and safflower may hit the 50-drop cap. "Rank the unsaturated samples from fewest to most C=C double bonds" (B.3) may not be supported by the data. Needs bench test (the instructor note already asks for one).
- **Part B framing:** B.3 says "a sample that takes more drops … contains more C=C double bonds" — true per unit mass only; oleic acid (a free acid) and triacylglycerols are compared per drop, which the intro frames correctly ("the same small amount"). Students are likely to copy the expected ranking (olive < safflower) rather than record it, because the intro tells them which is polyunsaturated.
- **Lotion stoichiometry (Q.7):** stearic acid 3 g ÷ 284.48 = 10.5 mmol; triethanolamine 1 mL × 1.124 g/mL ÷ 149.19 = 7.5 mmol → about 72% of the stearic acid is neutralized to triethanolammonium stearate (the emulsifier); excess stearic acid thickens. Consistent with Table 3 and Q.7. Expected lotion pH ≈ 7–8 (needs bench test).
- **Vitamin A (L10-08):** supplement capsules usually contain retinyl palmitate or acetate (esters) in oil — hydrolyzable — which conflicts with the intro's "fat-soluble vitamins … are non-hydrolyzable" if students classify what is actually in the tube.
- **Table 1 (L10-10):** "Sphingolipids: fatty acid, sphingosine, phosphate, amino alcohol" describes sphingomyelin; glycosphingolipids are also sphingolipids but are listed as a peer class.

## 4. Science and calculations

- **Pre-lab Q7 key:** 3.2 ÷ 7.5 = 0.4267 → **0.43**.
- **Q.2 example:** 2.25 g ÷ 0.325 g = 6.9 → 6 whole tablets.
