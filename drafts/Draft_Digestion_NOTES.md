# Draft Experiment: Digestion of Foods — notes for the authors

Source: `source/src/draft_digestion.js` → `drafts/Draft_Digestion.docx` (built with the house library; no new figures; reuses `fig11_2.png` Gly–Ala hydrolysis and `fig9_1.png` triacylglycerol formation).

## Summary
The lab follows a meal through the stomach and small intestine for the two foods the manual does not yet digest with enzymes: **protein** and **fat** (starch is already covered: acid hydrolysis in Exp 8 Part E, amylase kinetics in Exp 13; this lab only points back to them). **Part A** digests a milky suspension of cooked egg white at 37 °C in five tubes: pepsin at pH 2 (stomach), pepsin at pH 8 (pepsin after it reaches the intestine), boiled pepsin, acid with no enzyme, and pancreatin at pH 8 (intestine). Digestion is read as clearing of the cloudiness against a line card (levels 0–3, like the iodine levels of Exp 13). **Part B** starts with a 2-minute oil/water ± bile salts shake test (emulsification), then digests whole milk fat with pancreatin under phenolphthalein in dilute Na2CO3: with bile salts, without bile salts, boiled pancreatin, and bile salts with no enzyme. The pink fades as fatty acids are released; time to white is the readout. **Part C** is a short paper exercise (a cheeseburger through the digestive tract). Because the instructor asked for a "mock write up", a **Dry-Lab Option** (Instructor Note + clearly labeled SAMPLE CLASS DATA tables, one group plus six-group times) lets a section do the same report sheet and questions without bench work.

## Why each design choice
- **Egg-white suspension clearing (not egg-white cubes/Mett tubes):** cubes need many hours or overnight; a dilute heat-coagulated suspension clears with pepsin in ~10–20 min, which fits one period and gives a graded reading like Exp 13.
- **Line-card digestion levels 0–3:** mirrors Exp 13 Table 2, so students already know the logic; no spectrophotometer needed.
- **Pepsin at pH 8 (tube A2):** directly answers Exp 13 Q.5(b) at the bench and shows an enzyme denatured by pH.
- **Acid-only control (A4):** separates the two roles of HCl (denature, activate pepsin) from pepsin's hydrolysis.
- **Pancreatin for both A5 and B:** one enzyme bottle that *is* "pancreatic juice"; makes the point that the small intestine digests both protein and fat.
- **Bile-salts-with-no-enzyme control (B4) + B.1 shake test:** the key evidence that bile salts emulsify but do not hydrolyze.
- **Phenolphthalein in 0.05 M Na2CO3 with whole milk:** classic, cheap, color change visible in ~5–15 min; uses ideas from Exp 6/9 (ester hydrolysis, carboxylic acids are acids).
- **No biuret/ninhydrin on Part A:** pepsin products are still peptides (biuret stays positive), so the test would not discriminate; it is asked as a question instead (Q.4b).
- **Mock data are timed to the reading schedule** (Part A readings every 5 min, Part B every 2 min), so the sample numbers are ones students could actually have recorded; class averages are consistent with the group values.

## Estimated timing (≈ 2 h of a 2.5 h period)
- Pre-lab talk, baths heating, boiled pepsin (A.1): 15 min
- Part A set-up + 5 min warm (A.2–A.3): 15 min
- Part A readings (A.4): 30 min — do B.1 (5 min) and B.2 (boil pancreatin) during this time
- Part B set-up + warm (B.3–B.4): 10 min
- Part B readings + pH (B.5–B.6): 30 min
- Part C + cleanup: 15–20 min
- Pairs that split A and B between partners finish ~30 min sooner.
- Dry-lab version: ~60–75 min (report sheet + questions).

## Safety points needing EHS sign-off
1. **Enzyme powders (pepsin, pancreatin) are respiratory sensitizers** — draft has stockroom prepare solutions; students never handle powder.
2. **Phenolphthalein** is classified GHS H350 "May cause cancer" (Carc. 1B) and H341 (PubChem/ECHA C&L summary), and the indicator is in ethanol (flammable) — few drops per tube, gloves, away from hot plate.
3. **Waste routing:** Part A tubes (HCl/buffer + enzyme + egg protein) → aqueous waste; Part B milk tubes (phenolphthalein) and oil tubes → non-halogenated organic waste. EHS may prefer to allow neutralized Part A food/enzyme mixtures down the drain; the draft says no sink disposal.
4. **Food allergens** (egg, milk) and **animal-derived materials** (porcine pancreatin/pepsin, bovine bile) — draft tells students to notify the instructor and offers the dry-lab option.
5. 0.2 M HCl, 0.05 M Na2CO3, boiling water bath, stoppered-tube shaking — routine.

## Open choices for the authors
1. **Keep tube A5 (pancreatin on egg white)?** Egg white contains a heat-stable trypsin inhibitor (ovomucoid), so A5 clears more slowly than pepsin (mock data: ~25–30 min) and may not finish in some trial runs. Options: keep as is, raise pancreatin to 4%, extend A to 40 min, or drop A5 and let Q.1 rely on Table 1.
2. **Pancreatin vs purified lipase in Part B.** Pancreatin also contains proteases; peptide hydrolysis at pH ~9.5 releases a little acid too, so part of the fading in tubes 1–2 may come from milk protein digestion. The bile/no-bile and boiled controls still hold. Purified pancreatic lipase removes this, but some lipase products without colipase show a weak or even reversed bile-salt effect — run a trial before switching.
3. **Whole milk vs cream** (cream gives faster fading and a bigger bile effect but is thicker to pipette).
4. **Dry-lab only, bench only, or both** (e.g., bench for most sections, dry lab for sections without a 37 °C bath or for students who opt out).
5. Whether to add a biuret test of tube 1 vs tube 3 to show pepsin products are still peptides (adds Cu waste and ~10 min).
6. Placement in the manual: naturally after Exp 13 (Enzymes), since it relies on optimum pH and denaturation ideas.
